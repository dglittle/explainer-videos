Spinor v2: screenplay brainstorm

Oct 19, 2026 · for "make a v2 of my spinor video, with a voice-over" · 5 short scripts

In one breath: v1 (Spinor Belt Trick — 720° on a Chessboard) is an 8-second silent loop: 1080p60, the knight turns two full times clockwise in a single eased sweep, and the sheet balloons into a ball at 360° on the way. Nothing in it says "look, this is the halfway point", and that's the story v2 needs to tell. All five scripts build to the same beat: freeze at 360° ("the knight is back, but the sheet isn't"), then keep going ("same direction… and it's flat"). My pick is ① "Twice is nothing". It's simple, about a minute long, and needs only the existing animation plus a pause at 360°.

0. What's there

v1, frames every ⅔ s (0 → 720°):

Three looks already exist in your page variants, rendered here at 0°, 180°, 360°, 540° and 720°. Top: the board (bloop.monster/disk, two pawns; v1 is the same thing with the knight). Middle: spinor-rotation-board.html (Mar 8, the newest), where the board is hidden and only two belts (red on the d-file, blue on the e-file) are drawn. Bottom: human-ai/spinor.html, just one row of the board, with the light squares marbled red.

At 360° each belt makes a full loop over the top. That loop is Dirac's belt trick, and it's the 2-D board's secret seen one strip at a time.

Facts I checked in the shader, so the voice-over can say them safely:
• The edge of the sheet never moves. The center square (the knight's) stays flat and just spins.
• Every point of the sheet stays the same distance from the knight the whole time. Each ring of the sheet is only ever rotated, about axes through the center. So the sheet never cuts or passes through itself. It's a real, physical motion, not a trick of the camera.
• At 360° every ring has been flipped all the way over (a full turn about the left-right axis). That's the ball. At 720° every ring is back exactly where it started, so the sheet is flat again.
• Direction: seen from above, the knight turns clockwise, matching v1's description.
• v1's easing (t − sin(2πt)/2π) is slowest at 0° and 720° and fastest at 360°. That's the opposite of what a voice-over wants, so v2 will retime it (render.js-style time-warp to your recorded voice).

1. Five scripts

① Twice is nothing ★ my pick

~55 s · board + knight, existing animation, pause at 360° · easiest to make

0:00 Flat board, knight in the middle. Slow camera drift. VO: "This knight is standing on a stretchy sheet, and the edges of the sheet are pinned down." 0:06 The knight's square turns a little clockwise; the rubber nearby swirls. VO: "I'm going to turn the knight clockwise, and let the sheet around it follow along." 0:13 VO: "No cutting, and the sheet never passes through itself." 0:17 Turning. Swirl grows, sheet rises. VO: "Here's one full turn." 0:22 360°: the ball. FREEZE. Camera orbits slowly around it. VO: "The knight is back where it started. But the sheet isn't. It's wrapped all the way around." 0:31 VO: "And you can't smooth it back out without turning the knight back the other way." 0:36 Beat. Then it resumes, same direction. VO: "So let's turn it again. Same direction." 0:42 540°: the swirl winds down. 0:46 720°: flat. Hold. VO: "Two full turns, and it's completely back to normal." 0:51 VO: "Things that work like this are called spinors. Turn one once and it's tangled. Turn it twice and it's free." 0:55 End (or loop the 8 s v1 under the end card).

Note: "you can't smooth it back out" is the one claim we can't show with this animation. It's true (a single full turn can't be undone without unturning; that's the topology), but if you'd rather only say what's on screen, cut that line. ④ shows it instead.

② Try it with your hand (practical, viewer participation)

~60 s · board + knight · optional: a 5-second phone clip of your own arm doing the plate trick

0:00 Flat board, knight. VO: "Hold your hand out flat, palm up, like you're carrying a plate." 0:06 VO: "Now turn it clockwise, all the way around, without letting go of the plate." 0:12 (optional clip: your arm, twisted, elbow up) VO: "One turn, and your arm is all twisted up." 0:17 The knight turns once, synced: board climbs to the ball at 360°. Freeze. VO: "That's this. The knight is your hand, and the sheet is your arm, from fingers to shoulder." 0:27 VO: "Now keep going. Same direction. Elbow goes over the top." 0:32 Knight continues to 720°; the sheet unwinds. (optional clip: arm comes free) 0:40 Flat. VO: "Two turns, and you're untangled. Your shoulder never moved, and neither did the edge of the board." 0:50 VO: "One turn tangles. Two turns don't. Physicists call that a spinor."

③ One strip at a time (belt trick: board → belts → board)

~70 s · uses all three looks; needs a crossfade from board to belts (new work, but both are already drawn by the same deform code)

0:00 Full board, knight turns once to 360°: the ball. Hold. VO: "Turn this knight once, and the board wraps up into a ball." 0:08 VO: "To see why, let's look at just two strips of it." 0:12 Board fades away except the d- and e-files → the red and blue belts, still at 360°: two loops over the top. VO: "Each strip runs from one edge, under the knight, to the other edge." 0:20 Rewind to 0°, then play to 360° with only the belts. VO: "One turn puts a full loop in each belt. And you can't get rid of a loop by just pulling on the ends." 0:32 Continue to 720°. The loops swing over the knight and come undone. VO: "But a second turn, same direction, and the loops slide right off." 0:42 Flat belts. VO: "This is Dirac's belt trick." 0:46 Fade the whole board back in, play the full 720° again. VO: "And the board is just every strip doing it at once." 0:58 Flat. VO: "Twice around is the same as not turning at all."

This one teaches the most. It's also the one where the knight stops being the star, so it may suit two pawns (red + blue, one per belt, as on the board page) better.

④ Can you undo one turn? (the dare)

~60 s · board + knight; needs forward + backward playback

0:00 Flat. VO: "Here's a puzzle. I turn this knight once, clockwise." 0:05 Play to 360°: the ball. VO: "Now flatten the sheet out. You can stretch it and bend it any way you like, but the knight has to stay facing forward." 0:15 Play backward to 0° fast. Red X stamp. VO: "Turning it back doesn't count." 0:19 Back to 360° again. VO: "It turns out there's no way to do it." 0:24 Beat. VO: "But here's the weird part. Turn it once more…" 0:30 Play 360° → 720°. Flat. VO: "…and now it flattens out by itself." 0:38 VO: "So one turn is a knot, and two turns are no knot. Which means twice around isn't just 'once, twice'. It's a whole different kind of around." 0:50 VO: "Electrons live in that second kind. Turn one around once and it's flipped. It takes two turns to bring it all the way home."

The electron line is the standard popular statement (the quantum state picks up a minus sign at 360°). Keep it, or drop it if you'd rather not make physics claims.

⑤ 15-second short (vertical, loops)

~15 s · 1080×1920 crop of the board, camera a bit closer · cheapest

0:00 Flat. VO: "Turn it once." 0:04 360°: the ball. VO: "Tangled." 0:06 VO: "Turn it again. Same way." 0:11 720°: flat. VO: "Free." 0:13 Loop seamlessly back to 0:00.

2. Things to decide

• Knight or pawns? I'd use the knight: it has a face, so "back where it started" reads at a glance. The 85 MB mesh only costs anything on the web, not in a render.
• Which look? Board only (①②④⑤), or board → belts (③)?
• Length: ~1 min (①–④) or the short (⑤), or both (⑤ comes almost free once ① exists).
• Voice: same booth flow as "seen reddish-green?": I'll build a scrubbable 1920×1080 anim.html from the shader (ENGINE START/END), a booth with start/middle/end frames per line, and a render.js that time-warps to your take.
