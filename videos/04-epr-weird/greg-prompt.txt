"why is the EPR paradox weird? I've heard of taking an entangled pair of particles, and sending one off to alpha centauri, and then it's supposed to be weird that when Alice measures her particle, that Bob's becomes known, even if Bob hasn't measured it yet, but it for sure wasn't known.. they claim.. *before* Alice measured her particle. Oh no. Before that, it was in an unknowable state. And they're like, 'that's so mysterious right?', and I'm like 'why wasn't it known all along again?'. Well, here's why it couldn't have been known all along. We'll explain it with 3 increasingly true lies. The first goes like this: Alice and Bob bump a phone app that gives them each a button. Pressing it shows a 0 or a 1. And the app claims that mysteriously they'll see the same number, even if they press the button a lightyear apart. But, why couldn't the app just assign each phone the same number when they bump? Ok, fair enough, on to the second lie. Now the app has two buttons, and pressing either will remove both buttons, and show a 0 or a 1. The claim it makes is that if both people press the same button, they'll see the same number, but if they press different buttons, they'll see different numbers.. even if they're a lightyear apart when they make their selections! But, again, why couldn't the app just assign the same number to each button -- a different number to each -- when they bump? Then the prediction works out no matter what they each decide to press! Ok, fine, on to the third and nearly final lie: Now the app has 3 buttons, stacked like a traffic light. And now it claims, basically anything they press, they'll wind up with the same number, *unless*, one person presses the top button, and the other person presses the bottom button, *then* they'll have different numbers. Even if they're a lightyear apart. Now it really is tricky. When the app bumps, what assignment can it make? If it assigns a 1 to the top button (so that they'll both see the same number if they both press the top button), then it needs to also assign a 1 to the middle button (in case one person presses the top button, and one presses the middle). But then it needs to also assign a 1 to the bottom button (in case one person presses the middle button, and the other presses the bottom button). But.. that's not good, because now they're all assigned to a 1, and what do we do if one presses the top, and one presses the bottom? The only way we could do that is if we sent some kind of instantaneous message after one person presses their button, to the other app, letting that app know the situation. But that's hard when they're a lightyear apart. That's really the punch-line there. That's the spirit of what's weird about the EPR paradox. But the truth would be an app with 3 buttons, and everything is like we said if they press the same button, but if one person presses the middle button, and the other person presses the top or bottom, then it isn't a sure thing that the numbers will be the same, but there is a good chance, equal to cos(45 degrees), which is more than 75%. And if one presses the top, and the other the bottom, then there's a 50% chance they'll be the same. And.. it turns out.. such an app would also be impossible. Basically, if the top and bottom need to be completely uncorrelated (50% chance of being the same), then the middle can be at most 75% correlated with both the top and bottom (where 75% comes from being between 50% and 100%).
