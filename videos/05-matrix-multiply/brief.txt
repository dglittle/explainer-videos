The math. Greg doesn't know where he found it, and it has a name: **Falk's scheme** (Sigurd Falk, 1951; "Falk-Schema" in German
textbooks). Tell Greg, and offer it as an optional on-screen credit line. Don't put words in his mouth.
- A (m×n) sits bottom-left. B (n×p) is drawn ABOVE AND TO THE RIGHT of A, not straight above it: B's columns line up with the result's
  columns. The "implied space" bottom-right, under B and right of A, is C = AB (m×p).
- C[i][j] = row i of A (trace your finger left) · column j of B (trace up): multiply pairwise and sum.
- Free bonus: the shapes check themselves. The empty top-left square is n×n only when A's width equals B's height, and C's shape
  (rows from A, columns from B) falls out of the layout. Chains (A·B·C) make a staircase: the next matrix goes above-right of AB.
- Pick small integer matrices (e.g. 2×3 times 3×2, or 3×3) and have the page COMPUTE every number it shows, then check it headlessly.
  Animate the finger tracing left and up, the pairs lighting up, the products and their sum dropping into the cell.
