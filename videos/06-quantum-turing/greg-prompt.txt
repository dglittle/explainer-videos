"I'd like to give my understanding of quantum computation, for someone who knows about regular computation. So, with regular computation, we can do it all with a Turing machine, which has a long tape of 0's and 1's. To simply things, let's assume the tape is finite. Then we could do a step of computation in a rather funny way.. we could list all the states the tape could possibly be in.. which is a lot, 2 to the power of however many cells.. and we can imagine a vector that long, with 0's everywhere, except a single one at the position representing the current state of the Turing machine tape. And then to apply the next state update, we multiple this vector by a giant square matrix, such that the resulting vector has a single one at the position representing the new state of the tape after the update. Now to make this quantum computation, all we need to do is, instead of having 0's everywhere with a single 1, we instead relax the requiement to say that the length of the vector must be 1 (which is the same as saying that the sum of the squares of all the elements must be 1). Of course, actually the numbers can be complex too, and rather than squaring them, we want to get the magnitude by taking the complex conjugate (if that's correct). And we also require the matrix to be such that the result will also have a magnitude of 1. And this vector times matrix setup is sort of the Turing machine of quantum computation. Any quantum computation boils down to this." ? thanks!!
